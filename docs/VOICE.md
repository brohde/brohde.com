# VOICE.md — Brian's writing style guide

Living document. Every word published on brohde.com (and drafts by the copywriter agent) must pass this guide. Update it as Brian provides more samples; log changes in DECISIONS.md.

**Status: draft v1** — reverse-engineered from the 2026 resume, LinkedIn headline, the 2018–2019 brohde.com bio (via archive.org), and PLAN.md. Brian has offered more writing samples; fold them in when received.

## Voice in one line

A sharp practitioner writing quickly and well: first person, concrete, confident without selling.

## Traits (observed in Brian's actual writing)

1. **Compressed and declarative.** "AI multiplies my output; taste and engineering judgment decide what ships." Short clauses, often paired with a semicolon. No wind-ups.
2. **Metric-first proof.** Numbers do the persuading: "47-class pose classifier to 92.2% cross-validated accuracy," "detection rate 71% → 97%," "~20fps on-device." Prefer a real number over an adjective, always.
3. **Plain tech nouns, no hype.** Tools and stacks named exactly (SwiftUI, Extra-Trees, Core ML, WordPress VIP). Never "cutting-edge," "passionate," "innovative," "seamless."
4. **Conversational CTAs.** The old site's nav: "Read my bio," "View my work," "Start a conversation." Human phrasing over web conventions ("Start a conversation" not "Contact").
5. **Informal-pragmatic in prose.** PLAN.md register: "let's go with this," "worth highlighting." Casual is fine; sloppy is not.
6. **First person, active voice.** "I built," "I trained," "I shipped." Never "was responsible for."

## Mechanics

- **NEVER use en dashes (–) or em dashes (—). Anywhere.** Brian's rule, given twice (2026-07-15), including flagging them in design mockups. This covers prose, UI copy, navigation separators, metadata, and design comps. Use a comma, a colon, a middot (in metadata), or split the sentence. For ranges, use "to" ("2019 to 2021") or a hyphen in tight metadata ("2019-2021"). v1.1 update: the original draft treated em dashes as "avoid by default, confirm"; Brian's concept-04 feedback settled it, they are banned outright.
- Middots (·) are Brian's own separator in metadata contexts ("Founder, Eight Angle · Full-Stack Engineer") — fine in headers/meta, not in prose.
- Arrows (→) acceptable in technical metric contexts, as in his resume ("71% → 97%").
- Sentence case for headings. No title case except proper nouns.
- Oxford comma: follow resume usage (present); keep it.

## Banned outright

En dashes · "passionate" · "innovative" · "seamless" · "cutting-edge" · "leverage" (as a verb) · "responsible for" · corporate filler ("in today's fast-paced world") · unfalsifiable claims (any claim should survive "prove it")

## Test before publishing

Read it aloud: does it sound like a busy senior engineer explaining to a peer? Would every claim survive a skeptical client asking "prove it"? Is there a number where an adjective is standing in?


## Audience and positioning (Brian, 2026-07-15, binding)

Brian is going for FULL TIME ROLES as well as showcasing work for freelance clients. All copy, chat replies, and UI text speak to both audiences: hiring managers and potential clients. Never frame him as freelance-only. Prefer openings over obligations ("an unfamiliar tool is an opportunity to learn something new", not "a project detail").
