An aurora over the terminal, day and night, and a tide that carries a picture

Three of Brian’s ideas, built on the build.log ground. One aurora system, two grounds: a night terminal and a daylight one, and the stage opens on whichever your system prefers. The aurora drifts over the terminal like northern lights behind glass. The sky changes color when you move from the log to the chat: a cold aurora for reading, a warm gold one for talking. The glyph tide learns to render an image so real photos can live inside the character field. And the little paper planes overhead are the visitors. Demo 04 folds the sky, the tide and the glass into one scene and auditions three tide placements against the full homepage.

House rules, applied everywhere: devicePixelRatio capped at 2 · canvas ticks throttled · everything pauses offscreen and on hidden tabs · prefers-reduced-motion gets composed stills and instant state swaps · every text ink measured at the worst possible blob overlap, all AA or better on the glass, on both grounds.

01

Aurora over the terminal, two states, two grounds

The sky above the terminal is the interface mood. Home reads under a cold aurora (violet, teal, indigo). Entering chat warms the same sky to gold (amber, warm yellow, coral) over 2.6 seconds. One rose blob never changes: the thread that keeps both states one family. The whole system runs on two grounds, night and light; the stage opens on your system’s preference, and the ground toggle overrides it.

02

Glyph tide from an image

Brian asked: can the glyph matrix come from images I provide, so they stay somewhat visible? Yes. Luminance drives glyph density and weight; the tide keeps rolling through it, and the densest cells peak in phosphor mint on the night ground, deep green on the light one. Both sources here are drawn procedurally (no assets allowed in the lab), and real photos drop into the exact same pipeline.

# source drawn in code for this lab · your photo or video drops in here

canvas 2d text rasteroffscreen samplingPipeline. A 480×300 offscreen canvas holds the source. On resize it downsamples to one pixel per glyph cell (about 1,800 cells), one getImageData call, luminance cached. Each tick, every cell mixes its image value with the rolling wave and picks a character from the ramp: the figure draws in dense bright glyphs, the empty sky stays near black. 9fps on purpose; roughly 2ms a tick.

Real images. Swap the procedural drawing for drawImage(photo), done: same sampling, same cache. Best sources are bold and high contrast: a backlit yoga silhouette, a figure against sky, big shapes over fine detail. Video is the same call per tick, drawImage(video) plus one getImageData every frame: roughly 1 to 2ms extra at this grid, fine at 9fps, and worth a muted 10 to 15s loop rather than a full clip.

The control. Image presence blends the two fields: subtle keeps the tide dominant with the picture ghosting through; strong quiets the background so the figure carries. Reduced motion renders one settled frame at the chosen strength.

Two grounds. The night raster runs faint light ink up to phosphor mint #7EE787 at the densest cells: light burned into glass. The light ground mirrors it with a dark ink ramp (#14211A at rising alpha) peaking in the deep green accent #086148, 7.2:1 against the light ground, so the figure reads as ink drawn onto paper. Same buckets, same tide, only the ramp swaps; the ground follows your system preference, and either ground toggle switches both stages together.

03

Presence over the aurora

Live visitors as tiny paper planes drifting through the night sky in demo 01, each with a mono label: who they are and what they are doing, including one custom status. They are weather, not content: low contrast, slow, and they never enter the text column.

3 dom nodes + transformsThe mock, live in demo 01. Three planes wander bounded curves in the gutter to the right of the glass panel, 20fps transform updates, headings follow their velocity. Labels are the ground’s own ink at roughly half opacity (light ink on night, dark ink on light), dim enough to sit inside the aurora’s glow on either ground. The layer is decorative (aria-hidden, pointer-events none); the accessible equivalent is the status line: 3 guests overhead. The planes button pauses them, reduced motion parks them, and below about 760px the gutter disappears so they dock into the status line instead of crowding the column.

Real data. A Cloudflare Durable Object keeps the visitor roster; each client holds a WebSocket (or falls back to a 20s poll). Payload per guest: anonymous id, current page slug mapped to a verb (reading eight-angle, at the prompt), and an optional custom status set via a /status command in chat: length capped, links stripped, profanity filtered, and shown in quotes so it reads as the guest speaking. Cap the sky at 3 planes; beyond that the status line says n guests overhead. No accounts, no cookies, ids rotate per session.

Taste guardrails. Labels stay at 10.5px and roughly half opacity, planes never exceed walking pace, and the whole layer sits under the grain so it feels painted into the sky rather than stuck on top. If it ever competes with reading, it loses: one setting turns it off site-wide.

04

Aurora plus the image tide, one scene, three placements

Brian asked for the aurora and the glyph tide together, then asked whether the tide should cover the full background at all: with terminal text it might get busy. So this stage is the whole homepage (wordmark, bio, log, prompt) under one sky, and the tide auditions three placements: whisper strength across the full sky, full voice in the right gutter, or a contained hero band up top. The picture always fits whole inside the tide area: contained, centered both ways, never cropped, letterboxed with pure tide. Sources are per page (the Eight Angle logo mark and the pose for Eight Angle, f and G stand in for the Facebook and Google articles) and every source exits into pure tide before the next one forms. New here: a page theme is one identity. One press crossfades the sky, eases the ground and the glass to the theme’s own colors, retints the tide peaks, and travels the mark itself through pure tide, all on the clock the view toggle uses to warm the scene from cold to gold. And full sky now pours its glass lighter, so the picture ghosts through behind the terminal text instead of vanishing under it.

tide under the blobsgpu tint compositingluminance envelopeLayering. The tide canvas sits under the aurora layers and the glass sits over both. That order is the whole trick: the blobs are translucent blurred fields, so whatever color drifts over a glyph tints it in the compositor, for free, and the 2.6s home-to-chat crossfade recolors the wash with zero added JavaScript. Two alternatives lost on purpose. Sampling blob colors per cell means reading animated geometry from the DOM every tick and re-deriving a 64px blur falloff per cell: real cost, and it drifts from what CSS actually renders. Drawing the tide over the aurora keeps glyphs crisp but they occlude the sky and the field reads as a screen door. On top of the compositor tint, the tide speaks the state too: the three ink buckets stay a calm shared text ramp, and the peak bucket lerps between the page theme’s own home and chat colors (default: mint #7EE787 toward the gold tag #F5B968 on night, deep green #086148 toward brown #8A4D0F on light) on the same 2.6s clock as the sky.

Three placements, one worry: busyness against terminal text. Full sky used to hide its own picture: the glass poured at 78% and the tide sat at a whisper, so the terminal read as a solid card floating on atmosphere. Brian called it: the picture behind the window was invisible. So full sky now trades in both directions at once. The glass lightens to 66% on night and 74% on light (dark-on-light texture carries more edge contrast, so the light ground keeps the heavier pour), the default strength rises from whisper to subtle, and in exchange the script caps full-sky strength at a murmur: push the slider past it and the output reads sky cap. The pairing is measured, not felt: the cap is priced so that every ink still clears AA at the worst overlap through the lighter glass, and the glass panel remains the only surface text ever sits on. Without backdrop-filter support the glass falls back to near solid: no blur means no safe ghosting, so that one browser tier keeps the poured look. Gutter confines the tide to the right of the text column at full voice, where the presence planes fly: clean ground behind the column, and the canvas edge fades in with a mask so there is no seam. On phones the gutter has nowhere to live, so the tide becomes the full page background instead of a cramped band: same picture, contained and centered behind everything. Three things protect reading in that mode: the script caps the field to whisper strength no matter where the slider sits (the canvas holds near a third of full opacity and the image weight drops with it), the glass stays poured at 78/80% with backdrop blur, and the standing exclusion rule holds: text only ever sits on the glass, never on bare tide. Narrow the window with gutter selected to watch the fallback engage. Hero band contains the tide in a top strip that fades out before the content begins: the article-hero pattern from the placement discussion, calm ground below. Studio verdict: gutter wins for the homepage (the sky keeps the weather, the picture gets a stage, the text keeps a calm column), the hero band wins for article pages with each article’s own source, and full sky stays the capped option for moments Brian wants maximum atmosphere. Each placement picks its own default strength when selected; the slider stays live everywhere, and the two capped modes are exactly the two where the tide lives behind the whole page: full sky, and the phone background fallback.

Sources are contained, never cropped. Brian’s call: keep the whole picture. Sampling now contain-fits every source into the tide area, centered on both axes, scaled to the largest size that fits whole; where the aspect ratios differ the letterbox samples as pure rolling tide, so the figure floats centered in the field instead of bleeding off its edges. That holds in every placement: the landscape sources sit mid-height in the tall gutter, mid-width in the wide hero band, and dead center on the full sky. Nothing distorts, nothing clips.

Sources enter and exit through pure tide. Per-page sources are the confirmed goal: the Eight Angle logo mark (traced bold from the live eightangle.com header; the pose figure stays as a second option), an f letterform standing in for the Facebook Newsroom article, a G letterform for Android for Work. The stand-ins are drawn in code with the same white-ground black-figure pipeline as the 8; real brand assets can swap in later pending a trademark-use check. A source never swaps, it travels: the luminance envelope eases the picture back into pure rolling tide over 1.4s, the tide holds alone for 0.9s, then the next picture forms out of the same tide over 1.4s. The page theme buttons drive this same travel, because a page’s palette and its mark are one identity: the old mark dissolves while the old sky does, the tide rolls bare through the middle of the crossfade, and the new mark forms just as the new sky settles, the last thing to arrive. The source row stays live as a manual override for auditioning any picture against any sky (the pose stays reachable there). Press any source button to watch one full cycle, or set cycle to auto and it rotates through all five with a 4.6s dwell. Reduced motion skips the theater: source, theme and state changes render a settled still instantly.

Page themes: the whole scene belongs to the page. Brian’s confirmed goal is a color theme per page, and his second pass sharpened it: retinting the sky over the same green-black ground was too polite. So a theme now owns the ground it stands on. Eight angle turns the floor teal-black at night and the brand cream #F6F3EE by day, the same cream the live site sits on. Newsroom goes a deep Facebook-blue night (#0C1B36) and blue-cast paper (#F2F6FD), so the page reads blue before a single blob drifts past. Android for work deliberately keeps a neutral graphite floor (#101216 night, #F6F7F9 light): four brand hues on one ground read as a toy box, so the neutral floor lets the Google quartet in the sky carry the color, and the cold home sky pours a little stronger to compensate. The glass and the input well take each theme’s tint too, so the terminal never sits green on a blue night. Mechanics: the sky lives in two stacked slots and crossfades over 2.6s; the ground, glass and well ease their colors on the same clock under a class the stage wears only for the ride; the tide’s peak bucket lerps to the theme’s peak in step; and the mark travels through pure tide as described above. One press, one scene change. The rose anchor blob is never overridden: it is the continuity thread that keeps every page one family (android alone trades it for the quartet, flagged for Brian). Chat stays golden hour everywhere by design: page identity lives in the reading state, and the warm chat sky just takes the theme’s cast, so moving between pages never changes what talking feels like. Ground toggles and placement switches stay instant on purpose: the text inks snap with the ground, so the floor snaps with them.

Contrast, measured at the worst overlap, all four themes, both grounds. The audit got two new axes this round: the themed grounds and glass tints, and the two glass pours (the poured 78/80% that gutter and hero keep, and the lighter 66/74% full-sky pour with its strength cap). It still sweeps every theme, both grounds, both states, and six backdrop candidates per combination: the peak glyph and the dense ink mass, each with all four blobs stacked at center alpha, each bare, plus blobs alone and bare ground, all under the glass. The lighter sky glass moved the worst frame: it is no longer the bare night peak but the warm chat peak with the full blob stack overhead, seen through 34% transmission. Two inks had to move to hold the line, and both are scoped to this stage. The night secondary lifts one step, #8FA79A to #A2B8AB, because the old value bottomed out at 3.9:1 under the newsroom chat sky; it now reads 4.7:1 there, 4.9:1 under the default and eight angle skies, 5.5:1 under android, and 5.7:1 at the old worst spot. The light chat tag deepens from #8A4D0F to #7C440C, because the eight angle cream glass shaved the old brown to a 4.5:1 edge; it now clears 5.3:1 at its worst. Everything else holds without touching: on night, body ink #D6E5DC at 7.6:1 or better, headline #F2F7F3 at 9.2:1 or better, mint accent (also the focus ring) at 6.5:1 or better, amber tag at 5.7:1 or better in chat; on light, ink #14211A at 11.2:1 or better, secondary #3F5348 at 5.5:1 or better, green accent #086148 at 5.0:1 or better. The corner tag plate now takes the theme’s glass tint and measures 6.6:1 or better everywhere. Worst case across every theme, ground, state, pour and backdrop: 4.7:1. Everything AA or better, body inks AAA.

Cost, measured. Same 9fps cadence and cell grid as demo 02; the additions are one envelope multiply per cell, four color strings per tick, and the theme peak lerp, a handful of float ops per tick, not per cell. A theme change now does three things at once and none of them touch the tick: the sky swap is compositor opacity on the two slots, the ground and glass recolor is one background-color ease on solid rectangles (the backdrop blur under the glass is already recomputing every frame regardless, because the scene under it never stops moving), and the mark travel is the same envelope the source buttons drive. Re-measured in headless Chrome on the 1034×618 full-sky canvas (about 2,400 cells, slider pinned at 100) through a deliberately busy 22 seconds: four theme changes each carrying a ground ease and a mark travel, one manual source travel, a state recolor, a ground toggle and two placement switches. At dpr 1: 0.73ms average per tick, p95 0.9ms, worst 1.2ms over 157 ticks. At dpr 2: 0.77ms average, p95 1.1ms, worst 1.4ms. Budget is 4ms: the worst tick uses about a third of it. Zero runtime errors in every run, and the same scenario under forced reduced motion runs zero ticks: every change lands as a settled still. All five sources are sampled once per resize, not per tick. Pause, offscreen and hidden-tab behavior come from the shared runner; the motion button parks the blobs and the tide together. Production wiring is the same story as demos 01 and 02: placement, theme, ground and source are per-page (a class, a palette, an asset), state follows the route.
