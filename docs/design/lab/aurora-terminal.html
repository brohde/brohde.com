An aurora over the terminal, day and night, and a tide that carries a picture

Three of Brian’s ideas, built on the build.log ground. One aurora system, two grounds: a night terminal and a daylight one, and the stage opens on whichever your system prefers. The aurora drifts over the terminal like northern lights behind glass. The sky changes color when you move from the log to the chat: a cold aurora for reading, a warm gold one for talking. The glyph tide learns to render an image so real photos can live inside the character field. And the little paper planes overhead are the visitors. Demo 04 folds the sky, the tide and the glass into one scene and auditions three tide placements against the full homepage.

House rules, applied everywhere: devicePixelRatio capped at 2 · canvas ticks throttled · everything pauses offscreen and on hidden tabs · prefers-reduced-motion gets composed stills and instant state swaps · every text ink measured at the worst possible blob overlap, all AA or better on the glass, on both grounds.

The sky above the terminal is the interface mood. Home reads under a cold aurora (violet, teal, indigo). Entering chat warms the same sky to gold (amber, warm yellow, coral) over 2.6 seconds. One rose blob never changes: the thread that keeps both states one family. The whole system runs on two grounds, night and light; the stage opens on your system’s preference, and the ground toggle overrides it.

Two grounds, one system. Brian’s phrase was aurora on white, and the studio first built it literally: light ground, dark ink. The night read came later, and both survived review, so the site ships both: light and dark themes matching the visitor’s system preference. The stage above opens on your system’s ground; the toggle is a manual override, and until you touch it the stage follows live if your system scheme changes. Same sky geometry, same drift, same glass: only the ground and the inks swap.

Production wiring. State follows the route: log and article pages get the cold aurora, the chat screen gets gold. Same class toggle as this demo. Ground follows prefers-color-scheme exactly as this stage does: two custom property sheets, one class swap on the root, a live media query listener, no flash. Reduced motion swaps instantly and holds the blobs still; the content switch itself is always instant, a terminal jumps.

02

Glyph tide from an image

Brian asked: can the glyph matrix come from images I provide, so they stay somewhat visible? Yes. Luminance drives glyph density and weight; the tide keeps rolling through it, and the densest cells peak in phosphor mint on the night ground, deep green on the light one. Both sources here are drawn procedurally (no assets allowed in the lab), and real photos drop into the exact same pipeline.

# source drawn in code for this lab · your photo or video drops in here

canvas 2d text rasteroffscreen samplingPipeline. A 480×300 offscreen canvas holds the source. On resize it downsamples to one pixel per glyph cell (about 1,800 cells), one getImageData call, luminance cached. Each tick, every cell mixes its image value with the rolling wave and picks a character from the ramp: the figure draws in dense bright glyphs, the empty sky stays near black. 9fps on purpose; roughly 2ms a tick.

Real images. Swap the procedural drawing for drawImage(photo), done: same sampling, same cache. Best sources are bold and high contrast: a backlit yoga silhouette, a figure against sky, big shapes over fine detail. Video is the same call per tick, drawImage(video) plus one getImageData every frame: roughly 1 to 2ms extra at this grid, fine at 9fps, and worth a muted 10 to 15s loop rather than a full clip.

The control. Image presence blends the two fields: subtle keeps the tide dominant with the picture ghosting through; strong quiets the background so the figure carries. Reduced motion renders one settled frame at the chosen strength.

Two grounds. The night raster runs faint light ink up to phosphor mint #7EE787 at the densest cells: light burned into glass. The light ground mirrors it with a dark ink ramp (#14211A at rising alpha) peaking in the deep green accent #086148, 7.2:1 against the light ground, so the figure reads as ink drawn onto paper. Same buckets, same tide, only the ramp swaps; the ground follows your system preference, and either ground toggle switches both stages together.

03

Presence over the aurora

Live visitors as tiny paper planes drifting through the night sky in demo 01, each with a mono label: who they are and what they are doing, including one custom status. They are weather, not content: low contrast, slow, and they never enter the text column.

3 dom nodes + transformsThe mock, live in demo 01. Three planes wander bounded curves in the gutter to the right of the glass panel, 20fps transform updates, headings follow their velocity. Labels are the ground’s own ink at roughly half opacity (light ink on night, dark ink on light), dim enough to sit inside the aurora’s glow on either ground. The layer is decorative (aria-hidden, pointer-events none); the accessible equivalent is the status line: 3 guests overhead. The planes button pauses them, reduced motion parks them, and below about 760px the gutter disappears so they dock into the status line instead of crowding the column.

Real data. A Cloudflare Durable Object keeps the visitor roster; each client holds a WebSocket (or falls back to a 20s poll). Payload per guest: anonymous id, current page slug mapped to a verb (reading eight-angle, at the prompt), and an optional custom status set via a /status command in chat: length capped, links stripped, profanity filtered, and shown in quotes so it reads as the guest speaking. Cap the sky at 3 planes; beyond that the status line says n guests overhead. No accounts, no cookies, ids rotate per session.

Taste guardrails. Labels stay at 10.5px and roughly half opacity, planes never exceed walking pace, and the whole layer sits under the grain so it feels painted into the sky rather than stuck on top. If it ever competes with reading, it loses: one setting turns it off site-wide.

04

Aurora plus the image tide, one scene, three placements

Brian asked for the aurora and the glyph tide together, then asked whether the tide should cover the full background at all: with terminal text it might get busy. So this stage is the whole homepage (wordmark, bio, log, prompt) under one sky, and the tide auditions three placements: whisper strength across the full sky, full voice in the right gutter, or a contained hero band up top. The view toggle warms sky and tide together from cold mint to gold; sources are per page (pose for Eight Angle, f and G stand in for the Facebook and Google articles) and every source exits into pure tide before the next one forms.

tide under the blobsgpu tint compositingluminance envelopeLayering. The tide canvas sits under the aurora layers and the glass sits over both. That order is the whole trick: the blobs are translucent blurred fields, so whatever color drifts over a glyph tints it in the compositor, for free, and the 2.6s home-to-chat crossfade recolors the wash with zero added JavaScript. Two alternatives lost on purpose. Sampling blob colors per cell means reading animated geometry from the DOM every tick and re-deriving a 64px blur falloff per cell: real cost, and it drifts from what CSS actually renders. Drawing the tide over the aurora keeps glyphs crisp but they occlude the sky and the field reads as a screen door. On top of the compositor tint, the tide speaks the state too: the dense buckets lerp from phosphor mint #7EE787 toward the gold tag #F5B968 on night (deep green #086148 toward brown #8A4D0F on light) on the same 2.6s clock as the sky.

Three placements, one worry: busyness against terminal text. Full sky runs at whisper by default: image weight low, the whole canvas held near a third of full strength (dark-on-light texture carries more edge contrast, so the light ground needs the deeper cut), and the glass panel is the only surface text ever sits on; it is the most atmospheric and the most dangerous, so it gets the strongest restraint cap. Gutter confines the tide to the right of the text column at full voice, where the presence planes fly: clean ground behind the column, and the canvas edge fades in with a mask so there is no seam; on phones the gutter disappears and the tide slides to a band under the content. Hero band contains the tide in a top strip that fades out before the content begins: the article-hero pattern from the placement discussion, calm ground below. Studio verdict: gutter wins for the homepage (the sky keeps the weather, the picture gets a stage, the text keeps a calm column), the hero band wins for article pages with each article’s own source, and full sky stays a whisper-only option for moments Brian wants maximum atmosphere. Each placement picks its own default strength when selected; the slider stays live so any placement can be pushed to any strength.

Sources enter and exit through pure tide. Per-page sources are the confirmed goal: the pose figure for the Eight Angle article, an f letterform standing in for the Facebook Newsroom article, a G letterform for Android for Work. The stand-ins are drawn in code with the same white-ground black-figure pipeline as the 8 and the pose; real brand assets can swap in later pending a trademark-use check. A source never swaps, it travels: the luminance envelope eases the picture back into pure rolling tide over 1.4s, the tide holds alone for 0.9s, then the next picture forms out of the same tide over 1.4s. Press any source button to watch one full cycle, or set cycle to auto and it rotates through all four with a 4.6s dwell. Reduced motion skips the theater: source and state changes render a settled still instantly.

Contrast, measured at the worst overlap on both grounds. A picture under the glass changes the worst case, so this stage pours the glass stronger than demo 01: 78% night, 80% light (the no-backdrop-filter fallbacks are stronger still). The impossible worst spot is a solid peak glyph under all four blobs stacked at center alpha under the glass. Night glass, home state: headline #F2F7F3 at 13.2:1, body ink #D6E5DC at 10.9:1, secondary #8FA79A at 5.5:1, mint accent #7EE787 at 9.3:1. Night glass, chat state: 12.4:1, 10.3:1, 5.2:1, 8.8:1, and the amber tag #F5B968 at 7.7:1. Light glass, worst case is the darkest spot (dense glyph mass, no blobs): ink #14211A at 11.7:1 home and 11.8:1 chat, secondary #3F5348 at 5.8:1 and 5.9:1, green accent #086148 at 5.3:1 both, brown tag #8A4D0F at 4.7:1 in its own state. Everything AA or better, body inks AAA, on both grounds in both states.

Cost, measured. Same 9fps cadence and cell grid as demo 02; the additions are one envelope multiply per cell and four rgba strings per tick. Measured in headless Chrome on the software rasterizer, full sky at strong presence (a 1036×620 stage, about 2,400 cells): 0.50ms average per tick at dpr 1 (p95 0.7ms, observed max 1.3ms on the first tick), and 0.43 to 0.68ms average at dpr 2 including source transitions, the state recolor and placement switches, max 0.9ms. Budget was 4ms; the scene runs at roughly an eighth of it, and the aurora tint costs nothing on the main thread because the compositor does it. All four sources are sampled once per resize, not per tick. Pause, offscreen and hidden-tab behavior come from the shared runner; the motion button parks the blobs and the tide together. Production wiring is the same story as demos 01 and 02: placement is a per-page class, source is a per-page asset, state follows the route.
