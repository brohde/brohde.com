Aurora on white, a two state sky, and a tide that carries a picture

Three of Brian’s ideas, built. The terminal flips to a light ground with the aurora drifting behind glass. The sky changes color when you move from the log to the chat: dusk for reading, golden hour for talking. And the glyph tide learns to render an image, so real photos can live inside the character field. Little paper planes overhead are the visitors.

House rules, applied everywhere: devicePixelRatio capped at 2 · canvas ticks throttled · everything pauses offscreen and on hidden tabs · prefers-reduced-motion gets composed stills and instant state swaps · every text ink measured at the worst possible blob overlap, all AA or better on the glass.

pure css auroracrossfade state morphTechnique. Two stacked blob layers share one geometry and one set of drift keyframes; only the colors differ. Switching state crossfades the layers over 2.6s, so the sky appears to change hue while nothing repaints on the main thread. The terminal sits on a glass panel: 55% near white plus backdrop blur.

Contrast, measured. Ink #14211A holds 7.0:1 (dusk) and 8.8:1 (golden hour) even if every blob stacked at peak alpha on bare ground, which the layout never produces. On the glass panel, where all text actually lives, the worst spots measure ink 11.4:1, secondary #3F5348 at 5.7:1, green accent #086148 at 5.1:1. All AA or better, ink comfortably AAA.

Production wiring. State follows the route: log and article pages get dusk, the chat screen gets golden hour. Same class toggle as this demo. Reduced motion swaps instantly and holds the blobs still; the content switch itself is always instant, a terminal jumps.

02

Glyph tide from an image

Brian asked: can the glyph matrix come from images I provide, so they stay somewhat visible? Yes. Luminance drives glyph density and weight; the tide keeps rolling through it. Both sources here are drawn procedurally (no assets allowed in the lab), and real photos drop into the exact same pipeline.

source · strong ·

# source drawn in code for this lab · your photo or video drops in here

canvas 2d text rasteroffscreen samplingPipeline. A 480×300 offscreen canvas holds the source. On resize it downsamples to one pixel per glyph cell (about 1,800 cells), one getImageData call, luminance cached. Each tick, every cell mixes its image value with the rolling wave and picks a character from the ramp: dark areas draw dense heavy glyphs, light areas stay near empty. 9fps on purpose; roughly 2ms a tick.

Real images. Swap the procedural drawing for drawImage(photo), done: same sampling, same cache. Best sources are bold and high contrast: a backlit yoga silhouette, a figure against sky, big shapes over fine detail. Video is the same call per tick, drawImage(video) plus one getImageData every frame: roughly 1 to 2ms extra at this grid, fine at 9fps, and worth a muted 10 to 15s loop rather than a full clip.

The control. Image presence blends the two fields: subtle keeps the tide dominant with the picture ghosting through; strong quiets the background so the figure carries. Reduced motion renders one settled frame at the chosen strength.

03

Presence over the aurora

Live visitors as tiny paper planes drifting through the sky in demo 01, each with a mono label: who they are and what they are doing, including one custom status. They are weather, not content: low contrast, slow, and they never enter the text column.

3 dom nodes + transformsThe mock, live in demo 01. Three planes wander bounded curves in the gutter to the right of the glass panel, 20fps transform updates, headings follow their velocity. The layer is decorative (aria-hidden, pointer-events none); the accessible equivalent is the status line: 3 guests overhead. The planes button pauses them, reduced motion parks them, and below about 760px the gutter disappears so they dock into the status line instead of crowding the column.

Real data. A Cloudflare Durable Object keeps the visitor roster; each client holds a WebSocket (or falls back to a 20s poll). Payload per guest: anonymous id, current page slug mapped to a verb (reading eight-angle, at the prompt), and an optional custom status set via a /status command in chat: length capped, links stripped, profanity filtered, and shown in quotes so it reads as the guest speaking. Cap the sky at 3 planes; beyond that the status line says n guests overhead. No accounts, no cookies, ids rotate per session.

Taste guardrails. Labels stay at 10.5px and roughly half opacity, planes never exceed walking pace, and the whole layer sits under the grain so it feels painted into the sky rather than stuck on top. If it ever competes with reading, it loses: one setting turns it off site-wide.
