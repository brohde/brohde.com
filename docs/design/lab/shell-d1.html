The global shell: one frame, every page

This is the chrome every page of the site lives inside: the two-row global header (identity row, then the chat input on its own row, a real field at every width, per Brian's 2026-07-16 revision), the centered frame whose right gutter carries the scene on every page (#109), the per-page mark centered on both axes of that gutter and fixed to the viewport (#114), the drift running edge to edge behind everything, and the shipped site footer with the theme control (#91/#101). The left column is placeholder on purpose: page designs are phase D2 and D3. The type direction is DECIDED: editorial, the Circular face. The compact mono look stays available above as a comparison exhibit only. Both grounds are the exact production palettes, and the footer control is live: it cycles system, override, back to system, just like the shipped site.

House rules applied: sentence case for human copy, lowercase only for the wordmark, nav links and typed-command tokens (#76/#115) · zero em dashes (#18) · the interactive surface is "the chat", never anything else (#116) · every ink measured on both grounds, worst cases reported below · reduced motion parks the sky, the tide and the caret.

03

What D1 decides: the buildable spec

The numbers an engineer needs, so B2 can build the shell without interpreting a picture.

  • Frame (Brian, 2026-07-16). A centered container, max width 1200px, side padding clamp(20px, 4vw, 44px), holding [content column | right gutter]. The gutter is min(34%, 400px) of the frame, not the viewport: on wide screens the gutter rides the centered container while the scene runs edge to edge beyond it. The left column caps at 660px: room for the shipped 62ch prose measure with air around it. Below 880px the gutter drops and the phone treatment applies.
  • Scene layering, bottom to top. Sky (the lab's cold home aurora, both ground palettes), then ONE full-bleed tide canvas: a dimmed pure tide (ramp alphas baked to a third, the murmur register) behind the whole page, full voice inside the gutter. Then the glass: the page ground poured at 74% on night and 80% on light over the content region, aligned to the frame, dissolving across the gutter's first 90px. The column, the chat row and the footer never cross the gutter line, so text only ever sits on full-strength glass; the worst-case table below prices it. Grain on top of the scene, under the content.
  • The mark (#114). Contain-fit, centered on BOTH axes of the gutter, never cropped, viewport-fixed: the scene layers are pinned while the page scrolls past. The 8 is the default mark; per-page marks travel through the same slot (#111). Sizing lesson, measured in validation: the lab's landscape 480x300 source frame presents small in a tall gutter (the ink covered only 27% of the gutter's width), so the mock draws the 8 into a portrait frame trimmed toward its ink box, which is exactly #104's asset spec. The mark now presents at roughly 60% of the gutter's width. Production gutter sources should ship trimmed the same way.
  • Header, two rows (Brian, 2026-07-16). Row one is identity: wordmark brian rohde (lowercase, the home link, resting caret per #63) with quiet lowercase about and contact beside it. Row two is the chat: a REAL input at every width including phones, placeholder exactly "Ask me anything or type help" (#89), phosphor material, 48px tall, min(660px, 100%) wide and capped at the gutter line, so it aligns with the reading column like a composer.
  • Footer (#91/#101). Column width. Copyright "© 2026 Brian Rohde" left, the theme control right, hairline above, one flex row. The control keeps the shipped three-state cycle (#100) and the shipped label markup; it is live in this mock.
  • Type: editorial, DECIDED (Brian, 2026-07-16). Wordmark Circular Bold 18px, tracking -0.02em; footer copyright Circular Book 13.5px; everything machine-adjacent stays system mono (nav 13px, comments 12px, input 16px). The two Circular subsets embed here as data URIs; production keeps its self-hosted files. The compact mono look above is a comparison exhibit only and gets no further refinement.
  • Grounds. Token-for-token from global.css, including the override semantics: no attribute follows the system scheme; an explicit attribute pins either ground in both directions. The footer control and the lab buttons drive the same state.
04

Judgment calls, for Brian's veto

Every taste decision this phase made, with the reasoning. Any of these can be reversed cheaply now; they get expensive after B2.

  1. The chat wears the machine's material on both grounds. The input is always-night phosphor, the shipped homechip idiom grown into a field, not a theme-following well. Reason: chat is core navigation (#107) and the site's one distinctive object; a strip of the screen in the chrome gives every page the same doorway, and its fixed night ratios hold identically under either ground. The quieter alternative (a theme-following well like the base prompt) is one CSS swap if this reads heavy on light ground.
  2. The chat row is column width, not frame width. In the two-row header the input sizes to min(660px, 100%), aligned with the reading measure like a Claude or ChatGPT composer, leaving the gutter's air open above the scene. The full-frame alternative (input stretching over the gutter) read as a banner and fought the mark.
  3. The glass pour: 74% night, 80% light. Brian's full-background drift means text now sits over moving color, so the content region gets a translucent pour of the page ground, in the lab's glass spirit. The pours are the lightest 2% steps that keep dim ink at or above 4.98:1 at the lab's measuring bar (all four blobs stacked at peak over the tide peak, a backdrop the layout never actually produces). If Brian wants more drift showing through, the honest lever is the one demo 04 used: lift the scene dim ink a step (like its #A2B8AB move) and re-audit, rather than thinning the pour under the shipped tokens.
  4. The background tide plays at a third strength. The drift behind the page bakes its ramp down to the lab's murmur register while the gutter keeps full voice: one field, two registers, so the mark stays the picture and the page background stays weather. Running full voice everywhere buried the mark and made the skeleton shimmer.
  5. Nav links rest dim, not accent. about and contact are standalone nav tokens, not links inside running text, so the G183 color-only trap (#99) does not bind; dim holds 7.19:1 night and 7.97:1 light on bare ground and 4.98:1 or better on the glass. Hover and focus go accent with a 1px underline, the shipped hover grammar. Rest-accent made too many green objects in the header: the wordmark and the chat should out-rank the nav.
  6. The header is static, not sticky. Honest cost: the chat scrolls away mid-article, and the two-row header is taller chrome (about 120px). Sticky would tax phone vertical space and fight the fixed mark for stillness. If chat reachability mid-page matters, D3 can revisit (the article footer is the natural second doorway).
  7. The header input is 48px, not the homepage prompt's 60px. Chrome sizing versus hero sizing: the homepage surface is the page's object, the header input is furniture on every other page. Font stays 16px so iOS never zooms.
  8. The footer stays column width. The gutter is preserved at the page's end too: a full-width footer row would drag the theme control over live tide at full voice, where a borderless-background chip has no measured ground. Shipped pattern otherwise untouched.
  9. The comparison exhibit sets the copyright in mono, deviating from #91's "in Circular" inside that treatment only: its premise is no webfont anywhere. The decided editorial treatment honors #91 exactly.
  10. The wordmark keeps its caret in the header. The resting cursor (#63) travels from the homepage hero into the chrome, one identity everywhere; it blinks (parked under reduced motion) and would yield to the roving cursor in production. Cheap to drop if it reads busy at this size.
  11. Placeholder text shows at rest in this mock. The shipped input hides its placeholder until focus behind the boot typing machinery; the mock keeps the invitation visible because the shell here has no boot. Production keeps its own behavior.
